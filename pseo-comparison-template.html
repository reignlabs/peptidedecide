BPC-157 vs TB-500 for Tendon & Tissue Repair

At a glance

Both BPC-157 and TB-500 are marketed for tendon, ligament, and soft-tissue repair, and both rest almost entirely on animal data — credible human trials are absent for either as of 2026. Neither is FDA-approved, and neither is currently legal for U.S. pharmacy compounding. The most meaningful differences are TB-500's additional immune-modulating activity and each compound's distinct regulatory history.

What the current research shows

Objective language only — what the literature demonstrates, and what it does not.

BPC-157 · preclinical
The published clinical literature on BPC-157 indicates a pathway involving angiogenesis and tendon-fibroblast activity in rodent models. It does not establish efficacy or safety in humans; controlled human trials for tissue repair have not been reported.[1]

TB-500 · preclinical
Research on thymosin beta-4 and its fragment describes a mechanism involving cell migration and tissue remodeling, alongside immune-modulating activity. As with BPC-157, the evidence base is preclinical and does not support claims of treatment or cure in humans.[2]

Because neither compound has human efficacy data and neither is available through regulated pharmacy channels, the central question for a reader is not "which works better" but "what would justify either over an established, evidence-based option" — a question for a licensed clinician.

Considerations to raise with your doctor

If you have an autoimmune condition or take immunosuppressants: TB-500's immune-modulating activity is the more relevant of the two to flag, and warrants coordination across your specialists.

If you are over 40 or managing other conditions: the absence of human safety data means interactions and contraindications are largely uncharacterized — bring your full medication list to the conversation.

Frequently asked questions

Is BPC-157 legal in the U.S. in 2026?
BPC-157 is not FDA-approved. It was placed in FDA Category 2 in 2023 and, although removed from that category in 2026 after its nomination was withdrawn, it has not been added to the 503A bulks list and remains under review — it is not currently legal for pharmacy compounding.
What is the difference between BPC-157 and TB-500?
Both are studied for tissue repair, mostly in animals. BPC-157 is a synthetic gastric-derived peptide; TB-500 is a thymosin beta-4 fragment that also modulates immune activity. Neither is FDA-approved and human evidence is limited for both.
Do either of these have human clinical trials?
Credible human trial evidence for tendon or tissue repair is essentially absent for both as of 2026; the published data is preclinical.
